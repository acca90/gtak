# Plot: story spines to choose from

Status: **options** 2026-09-27. The user picks the spine (who the player is, how it ends, which mob wins);
screenplay-agent doesn't. All three use the same world: the Moretti-Orlov war, the Orchid Society and its
corrupt cops, the three love interests, and Commissaire Dick running for mayor.

Every spine keeps GTA's freedom: the three mobs' job chains can be played in any order, and the spine is
a thin layer of story missions on top that unlock at respect milestones.

## A. The Middleman

- **Premise.** The player is a nobody who arrives in Pastel City and works for whoever pays.
- **Act 1: Odd jobs.** Payphone work for all three mobs. Big Tony, Dimitri and Han each notice the new
  face. The love interests are introduced through their mob's jobs.
- **Act 2: Pick a side.** The war escalates (the bridge becomes a battlefield). Every mob asks for a
  loyalty job that hurts the other two; the respect bars force a choice. Commissaire Dick starts hunting
  the player to win the election.
- **Act 3: Endings.** One per mob the player backs to the end (Tony's family rules Side and Major City;
  Dimitri's syndicate takes the port and the bridge; Han's Society buys the whole city, cops included),
  plus a fourth: the player turns all three against Commissaire Dick and ends up owning a piece of each.
- **Why pick it.** Pure GTA2 structure, fits the respect system best, and is easy to build in slices.

## B. The Commissaire's Rat

- **Premise.** The game opens with the player busted by Commissaire Dick, who drops the charges on one
  condition: infiltrate the mobs and give him a big arrest before the election.
- **Act 1: Undercover.** Get hired by the mobs and report back to Dick by phone. He wants a boss, any boss.
- **Act 2: Double game.** The player gets close to the mobs (and one of the love interests) and learns
  that Dick is on Han's payroll and plans to hand the player over as the scapegoat.
- **Act 3: Endings.** Give Dick his arrest and live as his pet (the "bad" ending), or expose him with
  one mob's help and one love interest's plan (Katherine's case file, Lee's brother, Francesca's ledger),
  and Dick loses the election in the most humiliating way possible.
- **Why pick it.** A clear goal and a villain from minute one; the wanted level and the police become
  part of the story. Needs the wanted level (backlog 2) to feel right.

## C. Three Hearts

- **Premise.** Each mob has a succession crisis and a love interest at its centre: Francesca wants her
  father's chair, Katherine wants out of her brother's syndicate, Lee wants her brother out of Dick's jail.
- **Act 1: Three introductions.** The first job chain for each mob ends by meeting its love interest.
- **Act 2: Their plans.** Each love interest has a plan that needs the player, and the three plans
  collide: Francesca's peace deal needs the Orlov ledger Katherine is using for her escape, and both need
  the police files Lee is breaking into.
- **Act 3: Endings.** Whose plan the player completes decides the city: Francesca ends the war and runs
  the Morettis; Katherine vanishes with the Orlov money (and the player, if they go along) and the
  Syndicate collapses; Lee frees her brother and the Orchids break with Dick, who falls.
  The love interests make their own choices: a plan can succeed without the romance.
- **Why pick it.** The most character-driven; gives the love interests real agency. More writing, and
  the endings are about people rather than turf.

The spines can be mixed (for example A's structure with C's three love-interest plans as Act 2).
